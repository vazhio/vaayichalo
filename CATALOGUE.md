# Reading catalogue: acclaimed Malayalam writing and how you can use it

This is the shortlist for the site. Every entry says why the work is admired and what rights route it needs.

**Rights routes**
- **HOST**: public domain. Already on the site, or you can copy it in yourself.
- **HOST (India)**: public domain in India (the author died in 1965 or earlier). It may still be protected in other countries, such as the US. That is fine for a site aimed at Indian readers, but say so on the About page.
- **ASK**: still under copyright. You need the writer's or the estate's written permission, or a licence from the publisher (DC Books, Mathrubhumi Books, Current Books and others).
- **LINK**: you can't host the text, but the site can recommend the work and link to a legal copy.

India's rule is the author's life plus 60 years. So in 2026, anyone who died in 1965 or earlier is free. A new group of authors becomes free every 1 January.

### The legal shortcut: Sayahna
Sayahna Foundation (books.sayahna.org) publishes work that is either out of copyright or authorised by the copyright owner for free publication. K. Satchidanandan, for example, made his major collection തെരഞ്ഞെടുത്ത കവിതകൾ (1965–1998) copyright-free through Sayahna. **Read each licence carefully:**
- Public-domain works are usually CC BY-SA. You can host these with credit.
- Some items are CC BY-NC-SA, at the author's insistence. These are fine for a non-commercial site, but not if you ever add ads or paid tiers.
- Some catalogues, including Zacharia's, are CC BY-NC-ND (no derivatives). You may reproduce the text unchanged with credit, but you can't edit, excerpt into a new layout, or translate it. Ask the author to be sure.
- Satchidanandan's page on Sayahna says the original texts remain the author's copyright, and only Sayahna's encoding is CC BY-SA. Treat it as **ASK** until he or Sayahna confirms in writing.

### How to ask (a template you can adapt)
Write to the writer, the estate or the publisher. Say what the site is (free, non-commercial, one piece a day to build a reading habit). Name the one specific story or poem. Say you will show the full credit, a link to buy the book, and that you will remove it on request. Single short pieces are a much easier yes than whole books. Start with living writers who already publish on Sayahna, and with the Kerala Sahitya Akademi itself, which may help put you in touch with estates.

## 4. A good mix for the next weeks

- **Every week:** at least one Asan, Ulloor or Vallathol poem, one Aithihyamala legend, and one prose piece by a pioneer (Chandu Menon, Swadeshabhimani, A.R. Raja Raja Varma, Kesari).
- **Monthly serial:** run ഇന്ദുലേഖ or മാർത്താണ്ഡവർമ്മ in the story slot, one chapter a day, so the short-story pool lasts longer.
- **Keep each day under 30 minutes:** pair one long text with two short ones. The current week now tops out at about 31 minutes, down from 40.
- **Feature an award-winner:** once one permission comes through, mark it on the site ("Kerala Sahitya Akademi Award, 1960"). Readers respond to that signal.
